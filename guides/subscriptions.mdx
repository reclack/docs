A subscription starts when a customer completes a recurring checkout. They sign
one bounded authorization in their own wallet, and from then on Reclack runs
the billing against it.

## How one starts

You do not create subscriptions directly. You create a checkout session from a
recurring price, and the subscription is what the completed checkout produces.

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.reclack.com/v1/checkouts" \
  -H "Api-Key: $RECLACK_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "price_id": "price_7Fh2PqX9",
    "networks": ["base"],
    "customer": { "email": "dana@acme.dev" }
  }'
```

When the payment confirms you receive `subscription.created`, carrying the
subscription id, the customer, the amount and the next charge date.

## The authorization

This is the part worth understanding properly.

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Shape | A spending limit signed in the customer's own wallet |
| Size | A cap covering about twelve periods |
| Never | Unlimited. Reclack does not ask for an unlimited allowance |
| Revocable | By the customer, from their wallet, at any time |
| Enforced | A collection can never exceed the cap |

<Warning>
Do not describe the approval to your customers as unlimited access, and do not
build flows that depend on collecting more than the cap. If a plan grows past
it, the customer approves the new terms.
</Warning>

## Reading a subscription

```bash
curl "https://api.reclack.com/v1/subscriptions/sub_4Kd9" \
  -H "Api-Key: $RECLACK_KEY"
```

```json Response
{
  "data": {
    "id": "sub_4Kd9",
    "status": "active",
    "amount": "29.00",
    "currency": "USDC",
    "cadence": "monthly",
    "interval_seconds": 2592000,
    "network": "base",
    "chain_id": 8453,
    "started_at": "2026-08-18T22:07:41Z",
    "current_period_start": "2026-10-18T22:07:41Z",
    "next_charge_at": "2026-11-18T22:07:41Z"
  }
}
```

## Statuses

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `active` | Collecting on schedule. |
| `past_due` | A collection failed and retries are running. |
| `paused` | Retries were exhausted. No further collections until it is resumed. |
| `cancelled` | Ended. The customer sent it, or you did. |

## When a collection fails

A failure the customer can fix, such as an insufficient balance, is a schedule
rather than an incident:

1. The first attempt happens on the billing date.
2. Further attempts are spaced over the following days, not minutes.
3. A reminder email goes to the customer.
4. When the schedule is exhausted, the subscription pauses instead of retrying
   forever.

Your application hears `payment.failed` for the attempts and can decide what to
do about access. Most teams keep access during the retry window and only act
when the subscription pauses.

## Cancelling

Cancelling is the customer's authority under the contract they signed. When you
allow it, they send it from their own wallet through the
[portal](/guides/customer-portal), and Reclack confirms the chain agreed before
marking the subscription cancelled. Your application hears
`subscription.cancelled`.

<Tip>
Gate access on the event rather than on a nightly job. The event tells you the
chain has already agreed, which a job reading your own database cannot.
</Tip>

## Metered subscriptions

A subscription whose price is metered bills in arrears on what you record. See
[Usage billing](/guides/usage-billing).
