Reclack has a small object model, and most integrations only touch four or five
of these. This page is the map.

<Frame caption="The objects, and how one leads to the next">
  <img src="/images/object-model.svg" alt="Product has prices. A price builds a checkout session. A customer pays it. That starts a subscription, which opens an invoice per period, settled by an onchain transaction." />
</Frame>

## The catalog

A **product** is what you sell. A **price** says what it costs, how often it is
billed, and which networks it can settle on. One product can carry several
prices, which is how you offer monthly and yearly, or the same plan on
different networks.

```json
{
  "name": "Pro Plan",
  "price": {
    "amount": "29.00",
    "cadence": "monthly",
    "networks": ["base", "ethereum"]
  }
}
```

Prices are records rather than constants in your code. Changing what you charge
is creating a new price and pointing new customers at it, which leaves existing
subscriptions on the terms they agreed to.

## The people

A **customer** is who is paying. Give it your own `external_id` when you create
it, and you can find it later without storing our id anywhere.

Everything a customer does hangs off that record: their subscriptions, their
invoices, their payments and what they see in the portal.

## The payment

A **checkout session** is a hosted page your customer opens. It carries your
branding, lists the wallets their browser exposes, and explains the approval in
plain words before anything is signed.

Two other objects do a similar job in a different shape:

| Object | Use it when |
| --- | --- |
| Checkout session | Your application decides who is paying and for what. |
| Payment link | You want a URL you can share, with no code to deploy. |
| Invoice | You are billing a specific customer for a specific amount, with a document. |

All three end in the same place: a transaction onchain, settled to an address
you control.

## The recurring part

When a customer completes a recurring checkout, they sign one bounded
authorization and a **subscription** starts. From then on, Reclack opens an
**invoice** for each billing period, submits the collection against the
authorization, and follows the **transaction** until the chain confirms it.

<Note>
The authorization covers about twelve periods and is revocable by the customer
from their own wallet. A collection can never exceed it, and Reclack never asks
for an unlimited allowance.
</Note>

## Identifiers

Ids carry a prefix that says what they are, which makes a log line readable
without a lookup.

| Prefix | Object |
| --- | --- |
| `prod_` | Product |
| `price_` | Price |
| `cus_` | Customer |
| `chk_` | Checkout session |
| `plink_` | Payment link |
| `in_` | Invoice |
| `sub_` | Subscription |
| `txn_` | Transaction |

## Amounts

Amounts are decimal strings in the response, such as `"29.00"`, and are stored
internally as integers in the token's own atomic units. Floating point never
touches a figure that will be charged.

When you send an amount, send it as a string too. `"29.00"` is unambiguous in a
way that `29.0` is not.
